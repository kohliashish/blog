Good morning! It feels nice to get back into writing after a while—and what better topic to explore than the art of writing itself? Last week, while reading through a newsletter as part of my Sunday morning ritual, I found myself wondering - What would potentially be the motivation factor behind consistently writing a newsletter besides making it a lucrative billboard for ad publishers? It’s safe to say that curiosity took the better part of my weekend researching into popular newsletters and some research surrounding the topic.
{{< /dropcap >}}


### Personalization drives growth
The ever increasing list of newsletters, and the constant nudges by more and more content creators to signup for news letters hints towards the growing (or should I say consistent) popularity of newsletters. The curated experience of news letters makes it feel like they’re written just to your interests (at least all the good ones do). As opposed to the barrage of short form videos and posts on most popular social media platforms, there is joy in slowing down and reading through something substantial and consequential.

to make my case, I have numbers to prove! According to [Socialinsider Blog](https://www.socialinsider.io/blog/view-rate-of-instagram-reels-vs-stories/), the average view rate of an Instagram post is around 4.42%, and that of an Instagram reel is around 10%. On the contrary, the [email newsletters](https://www.smartinsights.com/email-marketing/email-communications-strategy/statistics-sources-for-email-marketing/) boast an industry average open rate of 36.5% indicating that emails drive better content consumption. Furthermore, according to [NY Times](https://www.nytco.com/press/the-new-york-times-sees-record-newsletter-subscriptions-and-open-rates/), people who read news via free newsletters are twice more likely to become paying subscribers as opposed to people who visit their website. Similarly, [Wired](https://www.wired.com/story/wired-paywall-one-year-later/) noted that visitors referred via a newsletter are 19 times as likely to subscribe as the organic visitors. Another similar study done by [Reuters research institute](https://reutersinstitute.politics.ox.ac.uk/sites/default/files/2022-06/Digital_News-Report_2022.pdf) correlates all the above insights, but one notable insight was weariness induced by content explosion among the audience. Notably, 42% of the survey respondents in the United states (38% Global average) said they actively avoid news or rely on social media platforms for anything factual. 36% of these respondents said their mood was negatively impacted and 29% said they were worn out by the amount of news. While this research was geared towards news consumption in general, several of the insights are also applicable to the world of newsletters.

### The Newsletter Hall of Fame
There are a few key names that resonate with most people when it comes to newsletters world. The [MorningBrew](https://www.morningbrew.com/), a prominent name in the news industry, began in 2015 with the success of a college side project by Alex Leiberman. Five years later, the company achieved significant growth and sold a majority of its business for a valuation of approximately $75 million. [Substack](http://substack.com/), a writing platform that revolutionized the newsletters space ever since its inception, has 2M paid subscribers (20M active) with it’s top 10 newsletters [collectively making over $25M a year](https://backlinko.com/substack-users). And that’s just scratching the surface - rhere are other prominent names in the Newsletter Hall of Fame (see the list of [Top 20 biggest newsletters](https://www.paved.com/blog/the-worlds-biggest-newsletters/))

### The silent hustle advantage
Besides offering higher engagement rates, newsletters also offer some obvious benefits to the writers:
1. **Lower startup costs:** You literally need a free email account to start! There are plenty of services which I have linked in the post below that offer scalability as you grow but you have to worry about all that later. 
2. **Ownership of audience:** With a newsletter, you own your audience (which is technically a list of emails of people who are waiting to hear from you on topics they love!). This removes any dependence on a platform, which is especially crucial in the times of turmoil where social media platfroms risk getting banned from countries due to geopolitical issues.
3. **Best in class ROI:** As per [SmartInsights](https://www.smartinsights.com/email-marketing/how-to-build-your-email-list-from-the-ground-up/), Email offers $40 for every $1 spent on marketing, which is the highest among all marketing channels and 80% higher than the next closest competitor SEO (which stands at $22 for every $1 spent).

### Writing in the age of AI?
As this point in the article, I’m pretty sure everyone would’ve had the question - “Okay but why bother?”. We’ve talked about how newsletters are the next gold rush, and why everyone wants in. However, competing with cutting-edge language models that can write your entire lifestory as a Shakespearean sonnet front and back before you’ve had a chance to blink, is like nailing jello to the wall. 

I think [Dr Jordan Peterson](https://x.com/jordanbpeterson/status/1413182150984208394?lang=en) has a simple answer to this - “Writing is thinking formalised”. We write to understand the world around us a little better, structures our thoughts and makes us articulate. The act of writing would never go extinct even when A.I. can write far more sophisticated arguments and compelling stories than humans.

What could be done instead is to leverage these language models as tools rather than thinking of them as competitors. There are plenty of tools that power your workflow as a writer and can help with the [blank page problem](https://www.forbes.com/councils/forbesagencycouncil/2023/07/20/generative-ai-and-solving-the-blank-page-problem/). From ready-to-use templates, to building AI agent powered automation workflows is made possible by tools like [Beehiv](https://product.beehiiv.com/), [ConvertorKit](https://kit.com/) etc.
